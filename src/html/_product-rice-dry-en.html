How rice (dry) affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

Dry rice is compact in carbs: 74 g per 100 g before it ever reaches the pot. Since 1 bread unit is 12 g of carbs, 16.2 g of dry rice makes up 1 BU. If your doctor follows the 10 g convention instead, the result will differ, so always check which norm is being used. The figures here are provided for orientation and are not tailored to you personally.

Rice has a GI of 70, which sits at the edge of the high band. Glucose may rise noticeably after a rice-based meal, especially when the rice is served on its own rather than with other foods. Individual response varies, however: the variety, the cooking and the rest of the plate all influence it. The GI is a reference value, not a medical forecast or a statement about your body.

Plan the portion while the rice is still dry: measure 16.2 g first, then cook it, because the finished grain is heavier and its volume is easy to overestimate. Rice pairs naturally with vegetables, pulses, fish or chicken, giving you a fuller plate in which the grain is not the whole story. Bread units are simply a handy way to notice carbohydrate amounts, not a rigid rule to follow blindly.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 g of rice (dry)?

100 g of rice (dry) contains 74 g of carbs. That is 6.2 BU at the 12 g norm or 7.4 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much rice (dry) makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 16.2 g of rice (dry). At 10 g it is 13.5 g.

What is the glycemic index of rice (dry)?

The GI listed for this food is 70 (high). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
