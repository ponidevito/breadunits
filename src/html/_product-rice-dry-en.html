Dry rice: varieties, starch and cooked portion traps

Dry white rice is carb-dense at 74 g per 100 g, making just 16.2 g equal to 1 BU. A typical 60 g dry serving totals about 3.7 BU and cooks up to roughly 160–180 g of fluffy rice.

The reference GI of 70 reflects standard polished white rice. Variety makes a real difference: basmati, parboiled and brown rice digest noticeably more slowly than sticky short-grain or risotto rice. Cooling cooked rice in the fridge before reheating also converts part of its starch into resistant starch.

Watch out for restaurant rice dishes such as sushi or stir-fries, where sweetened rice vinegar or teriyaki glaze adds extra carbs on top of the grain. Balancing a measured scoop of rice with vegetables, legumes, fish or chicken keeps the plate satisfying without overshooting your count.

Questions about this food

How does dry rice compare with cooked rice in bread units?

Dry rice has 74 g carbs per 100 g (1 BU = 16.2 g), whereas cooked rice absorbs roughly twice its weight in water and drops to about 25 g carbs per 100 g (roughly 45–50 g cooked per 1 BU).

Which type of rice has a lower glycemic impact?

Long-grain basmati, parboiled rice and brown rice generally digest more gradually than sticky short-grain or jasmine white rice.

How many BU are in 100 g of dry rice?

100 g of dry rice contains 74 g of carbs. That is 6.2 BU at the 12 g norm or 7.4 BU at the 10 g norm.
