White bread slices in practice: weight, crumb and pairings

Standard white loaf or sandwich bread packs 49 g of carbs per 100 g, putting 1 BU at 24.5 g. That equals one thin pre-sliced piece, whereas a thick bakery slice easily hits 40–50 g — close to 2 BU. Because an airy crumb looks light, weighing a single slice whenever you switch loaves prevents quiet undercounting.

Finely milled wheat flour has had most of the bran removed, so its starch breaks down quickly and produces a high GI of 75. Toasting does not erase carbs; it only drives off moisture, meaning 25 g of crisp toast actually carries more carbohydrate than 25 g of soft fresh bread.

Eating white bread bare or with jam leads to the sharpest glucose peak. Topping the same slice with eggs, cheese, avocado, smoked fish or crisp vegetables slows gastric emptying and keeps you full without reaching for a second slice.

Questions about this food

How many bread units are in one slice of white bread?

A thin 25 g pre-sliced piece is roughly 1 BU. A thick 45–50 g bakery slice works out to about 1.8–2.0 BU.

Does toasting white bread lower its bread units?

No. Toasting removes water, not carbohydrate, so one toasted slice keeps the exact same BU as before toasting (and has slightly more carbs per 100 g).

How many BU are in 100 g of white bread?

100 g of white bread contains 49 g of carbs. That is 4.1 BU at the 12 g norm or 4.9 BU at the 10 g norm.
