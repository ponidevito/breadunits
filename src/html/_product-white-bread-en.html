How white bread affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

When you plan a meal around white bread, start with the label figure: 49 g of carbs per 100 g. One bread unit equals 12 g of carbs, which means 24.5 g of white bread makes 1 BU. Some doctors work from 10 g instead, so check which convention your own specialist uses before comparing results. These values are reference only and do not replace individual guidance.

With a GI of 75, white bread sits in the high band. Glucose may rise fairly quickly after eating it, particularly when several slices are eaten on their own. That said, individual response varies with the rest of the meal, timing and everyday habits. The GI is a useful comparison tool rather than a fixed prediction of what will happen in your body.

A slice is easier to track than an abstract portion: weigh it once and set it against the 24.5 g figure to see how many bread units it represents. Pairing bread with vegetables and a protein food such as eggs, fish or cheese makes the meal more complete and gives you a natural reason to keep the slice count moderate. Treat the numbers as a starting point for awareness, not a strict rule.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 g of white bread?

100 g of white bread contains 49 g of carbs. That is 4.1 BU at the 12 g norm or 4.9 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much white bread makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 24.5 g of white bread. At 10 g it is 20.4 g.

What is the glycemic index of white bread?

The GI listed for this food is 75 (high). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
