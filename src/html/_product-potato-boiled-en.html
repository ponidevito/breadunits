Boiled potato: high GI, whole vs mashed, and cooling starch

Boiled potato holds just 16 g of carbs per 100 g, so 1 BU equals 75 g of cooked potato — roughly one medium egg-sized tuber. Unlike dry grains, potatoes are easiest to weigh once cooked.

Even with moderate carbs, boiled potato carries a high GI of 78 because cooking gelatinizes its starch quickly. Texture matters a lot: whipped mashed potato spikes faster than a whole skin-on boiled potato, whereas cooling cooked potatoes (for example in a potato salad) increases resistant starch and softens the glucose curve.

Serve boiled potatoes whole or in large chunks with skin on, alongside non-starchy vegetables, herbs, olive oil and a protein main like fish or chicken to keep the meal balanced.

Questions about this food

Why does mashed potato raise blood sugar faster than whole boiled potato?

Mashing breaks down cell walls so digestive enzymes reach the starch immediately, whereas whole skin-on potatoes take longer to break down.

Does cooling boiled potatoes really lower their glycemic impact?

Yes. Chilling cooked potatoes turns part of the starch into resistant starch, which slows absorption — though you still count the portion's weight for BU.

How many BU are in 100 g of boiled potato?

100 g of boiled potato contains 16 g of carbs. That is 1.3 BU at the 12 g norm or 1.6 BU at the 10 g norm.
