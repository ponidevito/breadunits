How boiled potato affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

Boiled potato is unusually light in carbs for a staple: 16 g per 100 g. At the standard 12 g per bread unit, 75 g of boiled potato makes 1 BU. Some clinicians use a 10 g norm instead, so the convention matters. These figures are reference only and can shift with the cooking method and the variety of potato.

Boiled potato has a GI of 78, which is firmly high. Despite the modest carb figure per 100 g, glucose may change quickly after a potato meal. Individual response varies with how well the potato is cooked, how it is cut and what is served alongside it. The GI points to a general tendency; it is not a medical forecast or a claim about any specific person.

Unlike grains, potatoes are easiest to assess once cooked: weigh the portion and compare it with the 75 g benchmark for one bread unit. A whole boiled potato with its skin, vegetables and fish or meat make a balanced plate without elaborate arithmetic. Because the GI does not describe the whole meal, treat the number as a guide to portion size rather than a verdict on the food.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 g of boiled potato?

100 g of boiled potato contains 16 g of carbs. That is 1.3 BU at the 12 g norm or 1.6 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much boiled potato makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 75.0 g of boiled potato. At 10 g it is 62.5 g.

What is the glycemic index of boiled potato?

The GI listed for this food is 78 (high). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
