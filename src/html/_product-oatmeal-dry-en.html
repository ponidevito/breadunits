How dry oatmeal affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

Oatmeal is measured in the bowl before any liquid goes in: that dry weight is what the count refers to. It contains 60 g of carbs per 100 g, and with 12 g of carbs per bread unit, 20 g of dry oats equals 1 BU. Some doctors use a 10 g norm, which will change the total. Treat every figure as reference only rather than as a personal prescription.

Oatmeal carries a GI of 55, which still falls within the low band. Glucose may shift more slowly than after foods in the medium or high groups, although individual response varies with the cooking method, the amount of liquid and whatever else is on the breakfast table. A GI value is a comparison between foods; it is not a promise of a steady sugar level and not a health claim.

Porridge expands dramatically as it cooks, so visual estimates are unreliable. Weigh 20 g of dry flakes first, then add water or milk and log that amount as one bread unit. Berries, nuts or yoghurt make the meal more varied and keep the oats from being the only carbohydrate source on the plate. Use the calculator to think clearly about portions, not to replace tailored advice.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 g of dry oatmeal?

100 g of dry oatmeal contains 60 g of carbs. That is 5 BU at the 12 g norm or 6 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much dry oatmeal makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 20 g of dry oatmeal. At 10 g it is 16.7 g.

What is the glycemic index of dry oatmeal?

The GI listed for this food is 55 (low). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
