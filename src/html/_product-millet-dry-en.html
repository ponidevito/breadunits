How dry millet affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

Millet is easy to overlook, yet dry millet contains 66 g of carbs per 100 g. With the standard 12 g of carbs in one bread unit, 18.2 g of dry millet makes 1 BU. If your specialist prefers the 10 g norm, the answer will be different, so any result from the calculator should be read as reference information rather than ready-made advice.

Millet has a GI of 70, placing it in the high band. Glucose may change noticeably after a millet meal, particularly if the plate holds little besides the grain. Individual response varies, though, and depends on the rest of the meal and your overall eating pattern. The GI describes how a food tends to behave in general; it makes no promise about what will happen to you.

Millet is usually rinsed and cooked until fluffy, but the weighing should happen beforehand: 18.2 g dry is one bread unit. A fluffy porridge looks light, which makes it easy to take more than intended. Add vegetables, cheese or fish so the portion feels complete rather than being just a mound of grain. Numbers are there to help you find your bearings, not to set medical rules.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 g of dry millet?

100 g of dry millet contains 66 g of carbs. That is 5.5 BU at the 12 g norm or 6.6 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much dry millet makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 18.2 g of dry millet. At 10 g it is 15.2 g.

What is the glycemic index of dry millet?

The GI listed for this food is 70 (high). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
