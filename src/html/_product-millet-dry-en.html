Millet: high-GI grain and how to balance a serving

Dry millet has 66 g of carbs per 100 g — almost identical to pearl barley (1 BU = 18.2 g dry). Yet the two grains behave very differently: millet has a high GI of 70, so a plain bowl of millet porridge raises blood glucose much faster.

Simmered millet swells to roughly three times its dry weight. Sweet millet recipes cooked with milk, pumpkin, honey or raisins can easily double the expected bread units if only the grain is weighed.

To soften its glycemic punch, rinse millet well and cook it fluffy rather than overly soft, then serve it savory alongside roasted vegetables, fish, eggs or herbs instead of pairing it with sugary toppings.

Questions about this food

Why is millet's GI 70 even though it is a whole grain?

Hulled millet cooks quickly and gelatinizes easily, allowing digestive enzymes to break down its starch faster than denser grains like barley or buckwheat.

How many BU are in a serving made from 50 g of dry millet?

50 g of dry millet contains 33 g of carbs — roughly 2.8 BU at the 12 g norm, before counting milk or sweet mix-ins.

How many BU are in 100 g of dry millet?

100 g of dry millet contains 66 g of carbs. That is 5.5 BU at the 12 g norm or 6.6 BU at the 10 g norm.
