Orange juice: fast liquid carbs (109 ml per BU) vs whole fruit

Orange juice packs 11 g of carbs per 100 ml, putting 1 BU in just 109.1 ml — less than half a standard glass. A full 250 ml glass carries 27.5 g of carbs (about 2.3 BU), and a large 330 ml café glass climbs to 3.0 BU.

Although its textbook GI of 50 technically sits in the low band, liquid juice has almost no fiber and leaves the stomach rapidly. It raises blood sugar noticeably faster than whole orange segments — useful when fast carbs are needed, but easy to overshoot as a casual drink.

Watch the label difference between 100% juice and fruit nectars or punches, which add sugar on top of the fruit. When you simply want citrus with breakfast, eating one whole orange (1.2 BU) delivers half the carbs of a glass of juice and far more fullness.

Questions about this food

How many bread units are in a 200 ml or 250 ml glass of orange juice?

A 200 ml glass has 22 g of carbs (~1.8 BU), while a 250 ml glass holds 27.5 g of carbs (~2.3 BU) at the 12 g norm.

Does freshly squeezed orange juice have fewer carbs than carton 100% juice?

No. Freshly squeezed juice still contains roughly 10–11 g of natural fruit sugar per 100 ml — virtually identical to unsweetened 100% carton juice.

How many BU are in 100 ml of orange juice?

100 ml of orange juice contains 11 g of carbs. That is 0.9 BU at the 12 g norm or 1.1 BU at the 10 g norm.
