How orange juice affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

Orange juice is not interchangeable with the orange entry. This liquid has 11 g carbs per 100 ml, so at the 12 g bread unit norm, 109.1 ml of juice equals one BU. Some clinicians calculate bread units from 10 g; keep that choice separate from this calculator. Because it is a liquid, measuring in milliliters is more reliable than pouring by eye.

GI 50 is in the low band, up to 55; medium runs from 56 to 69 and high begins at 70. Juice may still affect blood glucose because the carbs are in liquid form. GI is reference only, and individual response varies with the product and with what the juice accompanies.

Choose whole orange when you want the fruit record rather than this one; squeezing changes what is being counted. If you drink juice, include it with a meal rather than treating it as an invisible addition, and check the label if the drink is a nectar or sweetened blend. Plain yogurt or a savory meal can sit alongside it, but count each part with its own line.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 ml of orange juice?

100 ml of orange juice contains 11 g of carbs. That is 0.9 BU at the 12 g norm or 1.1 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much orange juice makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 109.1 ml of orange juice. At 10 g it is 90.9 ml.

What is the glycemic index of orange juice?

The GI listed for this food is 50 (low). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
