Honey: teaspoons vs tablespoons and why it still counts fully

Honey is heavy and carb-dense at 80 g per 100 g, so just 15 g makes 1 BU. A level teaspoon weighs 7–8 g (about 0.5 BU), a heaped teaspoon hits 10–12 g (0.7–0.8 BU), and a full tablespoon easily reaches 25–30 g — nearly 2 BU.

With a GI of 55 — right at the top edge of the low band because of its fructose share — honey is often mistaken for a blood-sugar-friendly swap for sugar. In carb terms it is nearly just as concentrated: 15 g of honey delivers the exact same 1 BU as 12 g of white sugar.

Because liquid honey clings to spoons, the cleanest way to measure it is placing the jar on a kitchen scale, hitting tare, and reading the negative grams removed. Keep an eye on glazes, dressings and hot tea, where honey dissolves out of sight.

Questions about this food

How many bread units are in a teaspoon or tablespoon of honey?

A level teaspoon (7–8 g) is about 0.5 BU, a heaped teaspoon (12–15 g) reaches up to 1 BU, and a full tablespoon (25–30 g) is roughly 1.7–2.0 BU.

Can I swap sugar for honey without counting bread units?

No. At 80 g carbs per 100 g, honey is almost as concentrated as table sugar (100 g) and must be counted in full.

How many BU are in 100 g of honey?

100 g of honey contains 80 g of carbs. That is 6.7 BU at the 12 g norm or 8.0 BU at the 10 g norm.
