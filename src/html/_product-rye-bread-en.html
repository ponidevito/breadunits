Rye bread: dense slices, sourdough and hidden malt

Rye bread carries fewer carbs per 100 g (42 g) than white bread, so 1 BU equals 28.6 g. The catch is density: a compact, moist rye slice that looks modest often weighs 40–55 g (1.4–1.9 BU). Judging it by white-bread habits usually leads to undercounting.

Its medium GI of 58 comes from soluble rye fiber and sourdough fermentation acids, which slow starch digestion. Watch out for supermarket dark loaves made mostly of refined wheat flour darkened with molasses, caramel color or malt syrup — those act much closer to white bread.

Check the ingredient list for wholegrain or dark rye flour in first place. Hearty rye slices pair naturally with cottage cheese, hummus, smoked fish, eggs or vegetable soups, giving strong satiety from a single weighed piece.

Questions about this food

Why can one slice of rye bread have more BU than a slice of white bread?

Even though rye has fewer carbs per 100 g (42 g vs 49 g), it is much denser. A heavy 50 g rye slice delivers 1.8 BU, while a light 25 g white slice is about 1.0 BU.

Is all dark bread genuine rye bread?

Not always. Many dark commercial loaves are mostly wheat flour colored with molasses or malt extract. Always check that rye flour is listed first and verify carbs per 100 g.

How many BU are in 100 g of rye bread?

100 g of rye bread contains 42 g of carbs. That is 3.5 BU at the 12 g norm or 4.2 BU at the 10 g norm.
