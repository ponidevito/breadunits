Dry vs cooked buckwheat: getting the portion count right

Dry buckwheat groats provide 62 g of carbs per 100 g, so 1 BU is 19.4 g before cooking. During boiling, buckwheat absorbs water and grows roughly 2.5 to 3 times heavier: 50 g of dry groats (2.6 BU) becomes a full 130–150 g serving of cooked porridge with the exact same carb total.

With a low GI of 50 and substantial fiber, buckwheat is a steady everyday side dish. Keeping the grains intact and fluffy digests more gradually than overcooking them into a soft mush.

When cooking a big pot for the whole family, weigh the dry groats once, weigh the finished batch, and split the total BU by the fraction on your plate. Buckwheat pairs well with sautéed mushrooms, herbs, roasted vegetables or grilled poultry.

Questions about this food

Why is dry weight more accurate for buckwheat than cooked weight?

Water absorption varies with cooking time: 50 g of dry buckwheat can yield anywhere from 120 g to 170 g cooked, while always containing the same 2.6 BU.

How many bread units are in one tablespoon of dry buckwheat?

One level tablespoon of dry buckwheat weighs around 18–20 g, which is right at 1 BU (19.4 g).

How many BU are in 100 g of dry buckwheat?

100 g of dry buckwheat contains 62 g of carbs. That is 5.2 BU at the 12 g norm or 6.2 BU at the 10 g norm.
