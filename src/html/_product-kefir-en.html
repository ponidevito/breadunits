How kefir affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

Kefir differs from milk in this table because it is measured with its own carb figure: 4 g per 100 ml. At the 12 g bread unit norm, 300 ml of kefir makes one BU. The 10 g variation used in some medical advice for bread units would change that result, so keep the systems separate. Kefir is a liquid entry, so milliliters matter more than a spoon estimate.

GI 25 is low under the scale used here, where 55 or less is low, 56 to 69 is medium, and 70 or more is high. Kefir may affect blood glucose because it still contains carbs. GI is reference only, and individual response varies between people and with what else is eaten.

Plain kefir matches this record; sweetened fruit kefir or flavored drinks should not be counted with the 4 g per 100 ml figure. Its fermented taste works in savory sauces and dressings without adding sugar, and it can accompany a meal rather than being treated as a separate calculation. Measure the amount in a marked container, especially when pouring from a bottle.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 ml of kefir?

100 ml of kefir contains 4 g of carbs. That is 0.3 BU at the 12 g norm or 0.4 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much kefir makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 300.0 ml of kefir. At 10 g it is 250.0 ml.

What is the glycemic index of kefir?

The GI listed for this food is 25 (low). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
