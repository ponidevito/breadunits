Kefir: why fermentation lowers carbs (300 ml per BU) and GI

Plain kefir holds just 4 g of carbs per 100 ml — slightly less than fresh milk because live kefir cultures consume part of the lactose during fermentation. That makes a generous 300 ml tall glass equal to 1 BU.

Natural lactic acidity combined with dairy protein keeps kefir at an exceptionally low GI of 25, making it a popular light evening snack or a refreshing base for cold cucumber soups and savory dressings.

Those figures apply strictly to plain unflavored kefir. Sweetened fruit kefir drinks often jump to 10–14 g carbs per 100 ml — triple the plain count. For flavor without hidden syrup, blend plain kefir with fresh berries, cinnamon or chopped herbs.

Questions about this food

Why does kefir have fewer carbs than regular milk?

During fermentation, kefir cultures break down part of the milk lactose into lactic acid, trimming carbs from ~5 g to ~4 g per 100 ml.

How many bread units are in a 200 ml cup of plain kefir?

A 200 ml cup of plain kefir contains 8 g of carbs, or about 0.7 BU at the 12 g norm.

How many BU are in 100 ml of kefir?

100 ml of kefir contains 4 g of carbs. That is 0.3 BU at the 12 g norm or 0.4 BU at the 10 g norm.
