Milk: one glass per BU, lactose and plant-milk differences

Plain cow's milk carries 5 g of carbs (lactose) per 100 ml regardless of fat percentage — skim, 2% and whole milk all land right around 240 ml (one standard glass) per 1 BU. A splash of 30–50 ml in coffee adds barely 0.1–0.2 BU, whereas a large 350–400 ml café latte reaches ~1.5 BU even before any syrup.

Its low GI of 39 reflects lactose paired with dairy protein and fat. Note that lactose-free cow's milk still holds the exact same 5 g carbs per 100 ml: the lactose is pre-split into glucose and galactose, making it taste sweeter and absorb slightly faster.

Plant milks vary wildly: unsweetened almond or soy milk often has just 0–2 g carbs per 100 ml, while oat or rice milk frequently climbs to 7–11 g per 100 ml — nearly double cow's milk.

Questions about this food

Does fat percentage (skim vs whole milk) change the bread units?

No. Lactose stays essentially the same (~4.7–5 g per 100 ml) across skim, semi-skimmed and whole milk, so 1 BU is always roughly 240 ml.

Does lactose-free cow's milk have fewer carbs?

No — it still has ~5 g carbs per 100 ml. Lactase simply splits lactose into simpler sugars, so you count the same BU.

How many BU are in 100 ml of milk?

100 ml of milk contains 5 g of carbs. That is 0.4 BU at the 12 g norm or 0.5 BU at the 10 g norm.
