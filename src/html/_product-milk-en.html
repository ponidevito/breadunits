How milk affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

Milk is measured by volume in this calculator, not by the weight of a piece. It contains 5 g carbs per 100 ml, so 240 ml of milk equals one bread unit at the 12 g norm. Some doctors calculate bread units from 10 g instead; if you follow that approach, use its own table. Kefir and yogurt have separate entries because their carb figures differ.

GI 39 places milk in the low band, up to 55; medium covers 56 to 69 and high starts at 70. Milk may affect blood glucose because lactose is a carb. That classification is reference only, and individual response varies with the type of milk and the meal around it.

Measure milk in a marked jug or cup rather than guessing from the level in a glass. Plain milk matches this entry, while sweetened or flavored versions need their own carb count because they are not described by 5 g per 100 ml. Drinking milk with a meal is a practical way to include it in the overall calculation instead of treating the portion as separate.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 ml of milk?

100 ml of milk contains 5 g of carbs. That is 0.4 BU at the 12 g norm or 0.5 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much milk makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 240.0 ml of milk. At 10 g it is 200.0 ml.

What is the glycemic index of milk?

The GI listed for this food is 39 (low). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
