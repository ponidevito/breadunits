How pasta (dry) affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

Pasta is counted before cooking, while it is still dry and easy to portion. Dry pasta contains 71 g of carbs per 100 g, and at 12 g of carbs per bread unit, 16.9 g of pasta equals 1 BU. Some doctors use the 10 g norm instead. Everything shown here is reference information and does not replace a calculation tailored to your own situation.

Pasta has a GI of 50, which belongs to the low band. Glucose may change more gradually than after high-GI foods, though the cooking time and the composition of the dish matter a great deal. Individual response varies even for the same portion on different days. The GI is a way of comparing foods; it is not a medical promise about your sugar level.

Measure 16.9 g of dry pasta first and only then boil it, because the cooked product is heavier and a mound on the plate is easy to misjudge. Tomatoes, vegetables, fish or chicken make good partners, so the sauce does not have to be the only source of flavour. Keep the dry-versus-cooked distinction in mind and use bread units as a practical bookkeeping tool rather than a strict ban.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 g of pasta (dry)?

100 g of pasta (dry) contains 71 g of carbs. That is 5.9 BU at the 12 g norm or 7.1 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much pasta (dry) makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 16.9 g of pasta (dry). At 10 g it is 14.1 g.

What is the glycemic index of pasta (dry)?

The GI listed for this food is 50 (low). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
