Pasta al dente: why cooking time and sauces matter

Dry durum wheat pasta carries 71 g of carbs per 100 g, so 1 BU equals 16.9 g dry. A standard restaurant plate often starts with 80–100 g dry (4.7–5.9 BU), which is why weighing 50–60 g dry at home (3.0–3.5 BU) makes portion control effortless.

Despite its high carb density, durum pasta holds a low GI of 50 because its tight gluten network slows down starch access. That advantage depends on cooking it al dente — overboiled, soft pasta loses that structure and raises glucose more rapidly.

Sauces can also shift the meal. Homemade tomato sauce with olive oil, chicken, seafood or vegetables adds flavor and slows absorption, while jarred pasta sauces often sneak in added sugar that belongs in your tally.

Questions about this food

Why is al dente pasta gentler on blood sugar than overcooked pasta?

Firm al dente pasta keeps more starch trapped inside its protein network, slowing digestion. Overcooking breaks down that structure and raises its glycemic impact.

Does whole-wheat pasta have much fewer bread units than regular pasta?

Total carbs are nearly the same (about 65–70 g per 100 g), so the BU count is very similar, though extra fiber makes whole-wheat pasta more filling.

How many BU are in 100 g of dry pasta?

100 g of dry pasta contains 71 g of carbs. That is 5.9 BU at the 12 g norm or 7.1 BU at the 10 g norm.
