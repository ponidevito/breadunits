Boiled corn: weighing off the cob vs canned sweetcorn

Boiled corn kernels hold 21 g of carbs per 100 g, so 1 BU equals 57.1 g of kernels. Because the inedible cob makes up nearly half the weight, a medium ear typically yields 100–130 g of kernels (roughly 1.8–2.3 BU). Weigh the ear before eating and subtract the bare cob afterwards for a clean count.

Tough kernel skins and fiber keep boiled corn at a low GI of 52. Whole kernels digest steadily, unlike finely ground cornmeal, polenta or cornflakes, which spike blood sugar far more sharply.

Canned sweetcorn is often packed in sweetened brine, so drain it well and check the label. Whole kernels work nicely in salads with black beans, avocado, grilled chicken or roasted peppers.

Questions about this food

How many bread units are on one medium ear of boiled corn?

A medium cob typically yields 100–130 g of edible kernels, which comes to roughly 1.8–2.3 BU at the 12 g norm.

Is canned sweetcorn counted the same as fresh boiled corn?

Drained canned corn is similar, though many brands add sugar to the brine — always drain it and check the nutrition panel.

How many BU are in 100 g of boiled corn?

100 g of boiled corn contains 21 g of carbs. That is 1.8 BU at the 12 g norm or 2.1 BU at the 10 g norm.
