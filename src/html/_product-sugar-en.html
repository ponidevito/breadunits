Sugar: the exact BU benchmark and where hidden spoons lurk

White or brown table sugar is pure sucrose — 100 g of carbs per 100 g — making it the cleanest possible benchmark: 12 g equals 1 BU (or 10 g under the 10 g norm). A single café packet or sugar cube usually weighs 4–5 g (0.3–0.4 BU), while a heaped teaspoon sits around 6–7 g (0.5–0.6 BU).

Sucrose has a medium GI of 65 because it splits evenly into glucose and fructose. Dissolved in hot tea, soda or coffee syrups, however, it leaves the stomach quickly — which is why measured sugar or glucose tablets are often used when fast carbs are needed.

Most miscalculations come not from the sugar bowl, but from packaged foods: ketchup, barbecue glazes, flavored yogurts, granola bars and coffee drinks. Check total carbs on beverage and dessert labels, and log all baking sugar before dividing a cake into slices.

Questions about this food

How many teaspoons of sugar equal 1 bread unit?

About 2 slightly heaped teaspoons (6 g each) — or roughly 2.5 to 3 café sugar packets (4–5 g each) — make 12 g, or 1 BU.

Does brown cane sugar have fewer bread units than white sugar?

No. Brown sugar is virtually identical in carb content (~98–100 g per 100 g) and counts the exact same way.

How many BU are in 100 g of sugar?

100 g of sugar contains 100 g of carbs. That is 8.3 BU at the 12 g norm or 10.0 BU at the 10 g norm.
