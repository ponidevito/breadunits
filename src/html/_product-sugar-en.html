How sugar affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

Sugar is almost pure carbohydrate: 100 g carbs per 100 g, so the arithmetic is clean. With 1 BU = 12 g carbs, 12 g of sugar is one bread unit, while some doctors use 10 g. Sugar has a GI of 65, in the medium band from 56 to 69; low ends at 55 and high begins at 70. Dissolved in coffee or tea, sugar may reach the bloodstream differently than in a baked dessert, so drinks are easy to miss. Use this as reference only; individual response varies.

Counting by taste is unreliable. A lightly sweet cake can contain more sugar than a spoonful in a latte, and a pale sauce may hide just as much. For home baking, write the sugar line before mixing, then divide the finished recipe according to how you serve it. Check labels on dressings and tinned fruit too, because sugar often appears under several names.

When swapping sugar for honey or syrup, recalculate instead of assuming the same weight equals one BU. Keep a small note of sweetened drinks beside the rest of your bread units, especially on refills. Dessert sauces deserve the same attention as the cake itself. The point is consistency with the 12 g norm, not a promise about what any single serving will do.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 g of sugar?

100 g of sugar contains 100 g of carbs. That is 8.3 BU at the 12 g norm or 10 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much sugar makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 12 g of sugar. At 10 g it is 10 g.

What is the glycemic index of sugar?

The GI listed for this food is 65 (medium). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
