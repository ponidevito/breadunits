How a pear affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

For pears, the main practical detail is that the database counts 10 g carbs per 100 g of edible fruit. Because one bread unit is set at 12 g carbs, 120 g of pear matches one BU here. If a plan calculates bread units from 10 g rather than 12 g, recalculate rather than mixing systems. The listed piece weighs 135 g, which works out to about 0.1 BU.

GI 38 is in the low range, meaning 55 or below; medium runs from 56 to 69 and high begins at 70. A pear may still influence blood glucose since it supplies carbs. Use that band as reference only, and remember that individual response varies with ripeness and with the rest of the meal.

Pears often change from firm to very soft as they ripen, and texture can alter how sweet they taste without changing this 10 g per 100 g database entry. Choose whole pear for the closest match to the calculator, and skip sweetened poaching liquids if you are tracking carbs. Plain yogurt or an unsweetened cheese is a simple pairing that keeps the focus on the fruit itself.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 g of pear?

100 g of pear contains 10 g of carbs. That is 0.8 BU at the 12 g norm or 1 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much pear makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 120 g of pear. At 10 g it is 100 g.

What is the glycemic index of pear?

The GI listed for this food is 38 (low). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
