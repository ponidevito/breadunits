Pears: 1.1 BU per fruit, high fiber and fresh vs poached

Fresh pears hold 10 g of carbs per 100 g of edible flesh, making 120 g equal to 1 BU. One medium 135 g pear delivers 13.5 g of carbs — roughly 1.1 BU, which makes a medium pear a very convenient one-unit fruit.

Despite tasting intensely sweet when ripe, pears sit at a low GI of 38. They pack even more fiber than apples (especially in the skin) along with naturally slow-absorbing fructose.

Fresh skin-on pears or oven-baked halves dusted with cinnamon fit cleanly into a daily log, whereas canned pears in syrup add substantial extra sugar. Sliced pear pairs wonderfully with walnuts, ricotta, blue cheese or arugula.

Questions about this food

How many bread units are in one medium pear?

A medium 135 g pear has 13.5 g of carbs — about 1.1 BU at the 12 g norm (or 1.4 BU at the 10 g norm).

Why does a pear taste sweeter than an apple yet have slightly fewer carbs?

Pears have lower acidity and a higher share of fructose, which tastes sweeter per gram than glucose, even at 10 g carbs per 100 g.

How many BU are in 100 g of pear?

100 g of pear contains 10 g of carbs. That is 0.8 BU at the 12 g norm or 1.0 BU at the 10 g norm.
