How french fries affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

French fries are a different proposition from boiled potato: 34 g of carbs per 100 g. With 12 g of carbs per bread unit, 35.3 g of fries makes 1 BU. A typical serving weighs 115 g and works out to about 0.3 BU. Specialists using a 10 g norm would get a different figure, so the numbers here should be treated as reference only.

Fries have a GI of 63, in the medium band. Glucose may change moderately after a serving, but a great deal depends on the cooking method, the thickness of the pieces and the fat content. Individual response varies from one person to another. The GI helps you compare fries with other foods; it is not a medical promise or a guarantee of a particular outcome.

Count fries as a whole serving rather than as a few stray strips: 115 g is roughly 0.3 BU, and that is the weight worth logging. A vegetable side plus fish or chicken turns the meal into something broader than fried food alone. This is not a ban on a favourite dish, simply a way to notice the carbs on your plate and make a deliberate choice about them.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 g of french fries?

100 g of french fries contains 34 g of carbs. That is 2.8 BU at the 12 g norm or 3.4 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much french fries makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 35.3 g of french fries. At 10 g it is 29.4 g.

What is the glycemic index of french fries?

The GI listed for this food is 63 (medium). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
