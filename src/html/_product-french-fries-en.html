French fries: why one serving is 3.3 BU and spikes late

Deep-frying drives out water, concentrating carbs to 34 g per 100 g — more than double boiled potato (1 BU = 35.3 g). A standard 115 g medium serving carries roughly 39 g of carbs, or about 3.3 BU, while a large 150–170 g box climbs to 4.3–4.8 BU.

Fries have a lower GI (63, medium) than boiled potato (78), not because they are lighter, but because frying oil slows stomach emptying. That fat-plus-starch combo often creates a delayed, prolonged glucose rise 2 to 3 hours after eating.

Dipping sauces matter too: a single ketchup or sweet-and-sour packet adds another 5–10 g of carbs (0.4–0.8 BU). When fries come in a combo meal with a bun and soda, log each item separately rather than eyeballing the tray.

Questions about this food

How many bread units are in a standard 115 g serving of french fries?

A 115 g serving holds 39.1 g of carbs, which equals about 3.3 BU at the 12 g norm (or 3.9 BU at the 10 g norm).

Why do fries have a lower GI than boiled potato yet more BU per 100 g?

Frying evaporates water, doubling carb density per 100 g (34 g vs 16 g), while the oil slows digestion and lowers the GI to 63 — often causing a later glucose rise.

How many BU are in 100 g of French fries?

100 g of French fries contains 34 g of carbs. That is 2.8 BU at the 12 g norm or 3.4 BU at the 10 g norm.
