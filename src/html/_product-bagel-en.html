Bagels: why size matters so much for bread units

Bagels are made from dense boiled-and-baked dough with 56 g of carbs per 100 g, meaning just 21.4 g equals 1 BU. A small 50 g bagel accounts for roughly 2.3 BU — and a large café bagel weighing 100–120 g jumps all the way to 4.7–5.6 BU on its own.

Refined wheat flour plus a compact crumb puts the GI at 72 (high). Eaten plain or with sweet spreads and a flavored latte, a bagel produces a fast carbohydrate load that rivals several slices of regular toast.

A practical habit is slicing a bagel open and eating half as an open-faced breakfast with cream cheese, smoked salmon, egg or leafy greens, saving the other half for later. Watch out for cinnamon-raisin or glazed varieties, which add extra sugar beyond the plain figure.

Questions about this food

How many bread units are in one 50 g bagel?

A 50 g bagel contains 28 g of carbs, which equals about 2.3 BU at the 12 g norm (or 2.8 BU at the 10 g norm).

How many BU are in a large 100 g bakery bagel?

A 100 g plain bagel has around 56 g of carbs — roughly 4.7 BU before counting any sweet fillings or toppings.

How many BU are in 100 g of bagel?

100 g of bagel contains 56 g of carbs. That is 4.7 BU at the 12 g norm or 5.6 BU at the 10 g norm.
