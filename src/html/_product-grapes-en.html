Grapes: why handfuls add up fast and fresh vs raisins

Fresh grapes pack 16 g of carbs per 100 g, making 75 g equal to 1 BU — roughly 12 to 15 medium berries, or one small handful. The real challenge is mindless snacking straight off the stem: a 300 g bunch quietly adds up to 4 BU.

Although grapes have a low GI of 46, their carbs are almost entirely simple sugars (glucose and fructose). In large amounts, the total glycemic load becomes noticeable even with a low GI.

Portion out a weighed 75–100 g bowl ahead of time instead of grazing from a full bunch. Never confuse fresh grapes with raisins: dried raisins concentrate to ~66 g carbs per 100 g (1 BU in just 18 g). Fresh grapes pair nicely with aged cheese or walnuts.

Questions about this food

How many individual grapes equal 1 bread unit?

One BU is 75 g of grapes, which works out to roughly 12–15 medium grapes (or 8–10 very large ones).

Do green grapes and red grapes have different bread units?

Their carb content is nearly identical (~15–17 g per 100 g across table varieties), so weight matters far more than color.

How many BU are in 100 g of grapes?

100 g of grapes contains 16 g of carbs. That is 1.3 BU at the 12 g norm or 1.6 BU at the 10 g norm.
