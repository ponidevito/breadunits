How pizza affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

Call it a slice, but count it as a plate: one typical pizza piece is 300 g and about 0.6 BU. The table lists 25 g carbs per 100 g, which makes 48 g of pizza equal to 1 bread unit at the 12 g norm; some doctors use 10 g. At GI 60, pizza sits in the medium band of 56–69. Its crust, sauce and toppings may all affect blood glucose, so the badge is a starting point, not a full description. Reference only; individual response varies.

Fast-food portions are rarely neutral. A breaded topping or sweet glaze adds another layer on top of the dough, and a dipping sauce can be sweeter than the tomatoes themselves. When you order, look beyond the cheese: ask what is in the sauce and whether the chicken is breaded. Counting only the visible filling is a common way to lose track.

At home, keep the 300 g piece intact in your log and divide the 0.6 BU only if you truly divide that piece. Add a salad or plain vegetables if you like, and choose an unsweetened drink rather than stacking a sugary soda on the same tally. Delivery menus change recipes, so recheck rather than relying on memory. The number is a guide, not a medical guarantee.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 g of pizza?

100 g of pizza contains 25 g of carbs. That is 2.1 BU at the 12 g norm or 2.5 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much pizza makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 48.0 g of pizza. At 10 g it is 40.0 g.

What is the glycemic index of pizza?

The GI listed for this food is 60 (medium). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
