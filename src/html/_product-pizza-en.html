Pizza: 6.3 BU per 300 g serving and the famous delayed spike

Classic pizza averages 25 g of carbs per 100 g, so 1 BU equals 48 g. A substantial 300 g serving — roughly half a medium pie — delivers 75 g of carbs, or about 6.3 BU. A single slice from a standard 30 cm pizza usually weighs 70–100 g, working out to 1.5–2.1 BU per slice.

Pizza is notorious for its delayed glycemic wave: even with a medium GI of 60, the pairing of white flour crust with generous cheese and oil slows stomach emptying. Glucose may look tame at the one-hour mark and then climb steadily 3 to 4 hours later.

Crust style changes the math quickly: thin Neapolitan bases carry fewer carbs per slice than thick pan or deep-dish crusts. Watch for sweet barbecue bases or breaded chicken toppings, and pair a couple of weighed slices with a green salad and water instead of soda.

Questions about this food

How many bread units are in one slice of pizza vs a 300 g portion?

A typical 80–100 g slice is roughly 1.7–2.1 BU. A full 300 g portion (about half a pie) contains 75 g of carbs, or 6.3 BU at the 12 g norm.

Why does blood sugar often rise 3–4 hours after eating pizza?

High fat from cheese and oil delays stomach emptying, spreading the absorption of carbs from the crust over several hours.

How many BU are in 100 g of pizza?

100 g of pizza contains 25 g of carbs. That is 2.1 BU at the 12 g norm or 2.5 BU at the 10 g norm.
