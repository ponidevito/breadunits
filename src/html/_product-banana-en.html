Bananas: why ripeness changes the curve and one fruit is 2.1 BU

Bananas are among the most carb-dense fresh fruits at 21 g per 100 g of peeled flesh, putting 1 BU at just 57.1 g — roughly half a medium banana. One typical 120 g peeled banana delivers 25.2 g of carbs, or about 2.1 BU, while a large 150 g banana reaches 2.6 BU.

The low GI of 51 applies to a just-ripe yellow banana. Slightly green bananas hold more resistant starch that digests slowly; once brown freckles appear, much of that starch turns into free sugars and raises blood glucose noticeably faster.

Because the peel accounts for roughly a third of the fruit's weight, always weigh the peeled flesh. Splitting a banana in half (~1 BU) and pairing it with plain Greek yogurt, cottage cheese or a small handful of almonds keeps a snack steady and filling.

Questions about this food

How many bread units are in one whole banana?

A medium 120 g peeled banana contains 25.2 g of carbs — about 2.1 BU at the 12 g norm (or 2.5 BU at the 10 g norm). Half a banana is close to 1 BU.

Does a ripe spotted banana raise blood sugar faster than a slightly green one?

Yes. Less ripe bananas contain more resistant starch and a lower GI, while heavily spotted bananas convert that starch into faster-absorbing sugars.

How many BU are in 100 g of banana?

100 g of banana contains 21 g of carbs. That is 1.8 BU at the 12 g norm or 2.1 BU at the 10 g norm.
