How a plum affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

Plums are small enough that the piece count can look like zero if you round too early. This entry lists 11 g carbs per 100 g and puts one bread unit at 109.1 g of plum flesh at the 12 g norm. The table uses a 40 g piece, and that piece is about 0.04 BU, not 0. Some practices calculate bread units from 10 g, but this calculator consistently uses 12 g.

GI 39 belongs to the low band, up to 55, while 56 to 69 is medium and 70 or above is high. A plum may affect blood glucose because it contains carbs. Treat the GI as reference only; individual response varies with ripeness, the variety, and the overall meal.

Because the stone is not eaten, weigh the edible part when you need a careful number. Soft, ripe plums and firm ones can taste quite different, yet the 11 g per 100 g figure is the one used here. Fresh plums match this entry; dried prunes belong to another calculation. Plain yogurt or unsweetened cheese makes a natural pairing without adding sweet syrup.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 g of plum?

100 g of plum contains 11 g of carbs. That is 0.9 BU at the 12 g norm or 1.1 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much plum makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 109.1 g of plum. At 10 g it is 90.9 g.

What is the glycemic index of plum?

The GI listed for this food is 39 (low). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
