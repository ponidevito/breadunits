Thin pancakes: 0.9 BU per crêpe and why fillings change everything

A classic thin crêpe-style pancake made with wheat flour and milk contains 28 g of carbs per 100 g, making 42.9 g equal to 1 BU. One typical 40 g pancake holds 11.2 g of carbs — roughly 0.9 BU, almost a clean 1 BU per pancake (two plain pancakes ≈ 1.9 BU, three ≈ 2.8 BU).

Its medium GI of 66 comes from refined flour and a touch of sugar in the batter. What really transforms the count is the filling: a spoonful of jam, syrup, sweetened condensed milk or chocolate spread can double the carbs of a single pancake.

Savory fillings — unsweetened cottage cheese, sautéed mushrooms, spinach with chicken, smoked salmon or cream cheese — add protein and keep the bread units right where the batter left them. For home batches, swapping part of the white flour for buckwheat or whole-wheat flour adds extra fiber.

Questions about this food

How many bread units are in one plain thin pancake?

One 40 g thin pancake has 11.2 g of carbs — about 0.9 BU at the 12 g norm (or 1.1 BU at the 10 g norm).

How do I count pancakes filled with meat or cottage cheese?

Plain meat or unsweetened cottage cheese adds almost no extra carbs, so the wrapper count holds. If sugar, jam or raisins are mixed into the filling, add those separately.

How many BU are in 100 g of thin pancakes?

100 g of thin pancakes contains 28 g of carbs. That is 2.3 BU at the 12 g norm or 2.8 BU at the 10 g norm.
