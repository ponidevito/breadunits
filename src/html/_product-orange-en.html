Oranges: 1.2 BU per fruit and why segments beat fresh juice

Peeled orange flesh contains just 8 g of carbs per 100 g, so a generous 150 g equals 1 BU. One medium orange (180 g peeled) holds 14.4 g of carbs — roughly 1.2 BU. Because thick citrus peel makes up 25–30% of the whole weight, always weigh the peeled segments.

Its low GI of 43 comes from the fibrous membranes holding the juice inside each segment. The moment you squeeze oranges into a glass, that fiber stays behind and a single 250 ml glass concentrates 2 to 3 oranges (over 2.3 BU instead of 1.2 BU).

Eating whole peeled segments after a meal — or tossing them into a salad with arugula, avocado, feta or grilled chicken — keeps blood sugar much steadier than drinking morning orange juice.

Questions about this food

How many bread units are in one medium orange?

One medium peeled orange (180 g flesh) delivers 14.4 g of carbs — about 1.2 BU at the 12 g norm (or 1.4 BU at the 10 g norm).

Why is a whole orange better than freshly squeezed orange juice?

Whole segments keep the fibrous membranes intact to slow absorption, while a 250 ml glass of juice packs the sugars of 2–3 oranges (~2.3 BU) without the fiber.

How many BU are in 100 g of orange?

100 g of orange contains 8 g of carbs. That is 0.7 BU at the 12 g norm or 0.8 BU at the 10 g norm.
