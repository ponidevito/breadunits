How an orange affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

Oranges are juicy, but this entry is for the fruit itself: 8 g carbs per 100 g of edible portions. With the standard 12 g bread unit, one BU corresponds to 150 g of orange. Some clinicians calculate bread units from 10 g, so keep that choice separate from this page. A piece weighing 180 g appears in the table as roughly 0.1 BU.

GI 43 sits in the low band, up to 55; 56 to 69 is medium and 70 or higher is high. Orange may still affect blood glucose because those carbs are still present. The GI figure is reference only, and individual response varies with the fruit, the meal around it, and personal tolerance.

To match this record, eat peeled segments instead of drinking the squeezed juice; the liquid format belongs to a separate calculation. The white membranes are part of the edible fruit in many tables, so do not assume that peeling more changes the listed 8 g per 100 g. Orange pairs naturally with plain yogurt or with a meal that also contains protein and fat, rather than with sweetened sauces.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 g of orange?

100 g of orange contains 8 g of carbs. That is 0.7 BU at the 12 g norm or 0.8 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much orange makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 150 g of orange. At 10 g it is 125 g.

What is the glycemic index of orange?

The GI listed for this food is 43 (low). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
