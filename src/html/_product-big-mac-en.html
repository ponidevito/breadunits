How big Mac affects blood sugar: portions and pairings

A Big Mac is assembled in layers, which makes the bun and sauce easy to overlook. One 215 g burger counts as 0.4 BU. With 20 g carbs per 100 g, 60 g of it equals one bread unit at the 12 g norm; some doctors use 10 g. Its GI is 70, the high boundary: high means 70 and above, while medium stops at 69. This burger may affect blood glucose through bun and sauce together, and the GI badge is reference only. Individual response varies.

Treatment of sauce is where habits differ. Extra sauce in the wrapper is still part of the meal, and switching to a breaded patty means using that product's line instead of carrying over 0.4 BU. Menu names stay familiar while recipes change, so look at the current description before you log anything.

Keep the burger, side and drink in separate columns. Fries, breaded bites or a sweet soda do not become carb-free because they share a tray with the burger. If you skip the pickles or use less sauce, the bun is still there in the count. The 0.4 BU figure belongs to the 215 g burger alone; use it as a planning reference rather than a promise about your own glucose curve.

Questions about this food

How many BU are in 100 g of big Mac?

100 g of big Mac contains 20 g of carbs. That is 1.7 BU at the 12 g norm or 2 BU at the 10 g norm.

How much big Mac makes 1 BU?

At the 12 g carb norm, 1 BU corresponds to 60 g of big Mac. At 10 g it is 50 g.

What is the glycemic index of big Mac?

The GI listed for this food is 70 (high). It is an approximate reference value, not an individual recommendation.
